Synthesis of High Mechanical Strength and Thermally
Recyclable and Reversible Polyurethane Adhesive by
Diels–Alder Reaction
Jian Xi and Niangui Wang*
Recyclability of polyurethane materials is signiﬁcant to relieve environmental
problems caused by damaged polymers. Inspired by plenty of self-healing
properties based on dynamic covalent bonds. A high mechanical strength and
thermally reversible polyurethane adhesive are acquired through
co-polymerization of poly-1,4-butylene adipate glycol (PBA), soybean oil-based
polyol (MESO), and toluene diisocyanate (TDI) whose linear polymer chains
are constructed by Diels–Alder reaction between furfuryl alcohol (FA) and
bismaleimide (BMI), named DAPU. Further, the obtained polyurethane
adhesives show great recyclability, mechanical performance (Whose tensile
strength can reach 91.7 MPa), and appropriate self-healing ability through the
thermally reversible Diels–Alder covalent bonds and hydrogen bonds between
urethane groups, which may pave a way for further development of recyclable
materials.
1. Introduction
Polyurethane adhesives have excellent adhesion performance,
simple operation, and no side reaction during curing, which thus
have been widely used in the bonding of metal, wood, leather,
plastic, and rubber.[1] However, with the generation of micro-
cracks during the use of polyurethane adhesives, they could ef-
fect even shorted the service life of polymer materials. If these
cracks are not ﬁxed timely, they will lead to damage and even
safety accidents.[2–4] Thus protective measures must be taken
when cracks occur. Peculiar adhesives necessitate multiple func-
tionalities to adjust to special environmental demands. Inspired
by many natural materials that possess their own self-healing
properties and could repair themselves after stimulation. A large
plenty of self-healing polymers have been increased recently.
Most importantly, these intelligent self-healing polymer materi-
als could achieve multiple reversible repairs through reversible
fracture recombination or dynamic exchange in their system
then eliminating hazards and extending service life.[5–8] In addi-
tion, due to the accumulation of polyurethane adhesive garbage,

With more and more attention the
self-healing polymers have received, they
could be divided into two categories: the
healing process needs additional healing
agents encapsulated in a polymer ma-
trix and the healing process relies on
incorporating covalent bond or non-covalent interaction into a
polymer system without any other agents.[12] The reversible dy-
namic covalent bonds including imide bonds, DA reactions,[13]
boronate ester[14], and disulﬁde[15] which could form or break re-
versibly through stimuli such as heat, light,[16] and thermal.[17]
These properties allow dynamic polymer cross-linking groups to
modify continuously through recombination and exchange. Nev-
ertheless, the appropriate healing reaction temperature as well as
the speed and eﬃciency of the healing process are still signiﬁcant
challenges.[18]
Up to the present, several eﬀorts have been prepared to in-
vestigate recyclable polyurethane materials based on dynamic co-
valent bonds. For example, Shan[19] obtained a novel hydropho-
bic chain extender containing disulﬁde bonds into polymer
backbones. Although this hydrophobic self-healing waterborne
polyurethane (deﬁned as WPU) contributed to the scratch resis-
tance, and reprocess ability, its tensile breaking strength could
only reach 12.79 MPa which may further inﬂuence the feasibil-
ity of practical applications. Sai[20] reported a thermal-driven self-
healing waterborne polyurethane ﬁlms based on dynamic oxime-
carbamate and DA bonds. The WPU ﬁlms showed strong self-
healing capability and the self-healing eﬃciency of its mechani-
cal properties could reach 92.9 ± 0.1% MPa after self-healing at
120 °C for 4 h. In addition, its mechanical properties and thermal
stability of the WPU ﬁlms were signiﬁcantly improved after the
introduction of oxime-carbamate and DA bonds. Hence it is still
a challenge to acquire high-performance polyurethane adhesives
while maintaining excellent recyclability and self-healable ability.

2.2. Synthesis of DA-Diol
First, FA(5.13 g, 48 mmol) was dissolved in anhydrous acetone
in a 250 ml three-necked round-bottom ﬂask. The reaction was
conducted at a reﬂux temperature(≈65 °C) under mechanical stir-
ring. Then, a solution of BMI(7.17 g, 20 mmol) was added drop-
wise to the above mixture. After stirring for 24 h, the obtained so-
lution was cooled to room temperature and collected after remov-
ing the solvent using a rotary evaporator. Subsequently, the con-
centrated solution was added into ethyl ether to obtain buﬀpre-
cipitates. Finally, the precipitates were washed three times with
acetone and ethyl ether and dried in a vacuum.
2.3. Synthesis of DAPU
TDI, MESO, and PBA were added in a 100 ml three-necked
round-bottom ﬂask with mechanical stirring and reﬂuxing, as il-
lustrated in Figure 1b. After that two drops of DBTDL were added
and the solution was kept stirring at 80 °C for 2.5 h. Then BDO
was added and the solution was further stirred for 1 h. Afterward,
DA-diol was added and the system was kept stirring for 4 h to ob-
tain yellow DA polyurethane adhesive. Finally, the obtained prod-
uct was spread on an iron plate and dried at room temperature
overnight.

3. Results and Discussion
3.1. Characterization of DAdiol and DAPU
As shown in Figure 2, DAdiol was ﬁrst prepared by Diels–Alder
clicks reaction of FA with BMI. From the FT-IR spectra of DA-
diol (Figure 2a), the characteristic absorption peaks of the furan
ring of FA at 630 and 810 cm−1 disappeared while the charac-
teristic absorption peak of BMI of C─H connected with C═C at
686 cm−1 and the characteristic absorption peak of C─N─C at
1146 cm−1 disappeared, while the vibration peak of C═C at 1176
cm−1and the absorption peak of C─C at 1193 cm−1 of DA adducts
emerged.[21–24] These results conﬁrmed the successful prepara-
tion of the functional chain extender that contains Diels–Alder
dynamic covalent bonds. In the second stage, a series of new
polyurethane adhesives were synthesized through the chemical
reaction of MESO, PBA, and TDI in diﬀerent ratios. (Table S1,
Supporting Information) As depicted in Figure 2b, there were no
obvious characteristic vibration peaks of -NCO at 2270 cm−1 and
-OH at 3453 cm−1. The vibration peaks of the urethane group at
3343 and 1533 cm−1 emerged which suggested that the copoly-
merization of the three monomers was completed. In addition,
with the appearance of the C─C vibration peak of DA adducts at
1183 cm−1 and the C─N─C vibration peak of DA adducts at 1538
cm−1,[25,26] it can be conﬁrmed that the cross-linked polyurethane
that containing DA characteristic groups have obtained success-
fully.
3.2. Thermal Properties of DAPU
To further investigate the eﬀect of dynamic DA bonds on the
properties of polyurethane materials. DAdiol containing 0%, 5%,
10%, 15% and 25%, respectively was introduced into the poly-

15 and DAPU-25. Diﬀerential scanning calorimetry (DSC) anal-
ysis (Figure 3a) of the DAPU suggested that with the incorpo-
ration of the DA covalent bonds, the DAPU adhesives showed
only one obvious glass transition temperature (Tg), suggesting
that DAdiol was evenly distributed in polyurethane materials and
there was no apparent phase separation phenomenon. In addi-
tion, from the ﬁgure 3a, it can be discovered that compared with
the polyurethane adhesives without any DA groups, DAPU-25

3.3. Mechanical and Self-Healing Properties of DAPU
The mechanical properties of the polyurethane adhesives with
diﬀerent ratios of DAdiol were measured on a universal testing
machine, and each sample was tested three times. As shown
in Figure 3b, the stress-strain curves of the polyurethane adhe-
sives at room temperature exhibited an increasing trend of ten-
sile strength with the addition of DAdiol. Compared with the
polyurethane adhesives without DAdiol, by modifying the ratio of
DAdiol, the polyurethane adhesives showed remarkably tunable
mechanical properties. When the DAdiol content was 10 mol%,
the tensile strength increased from 43.38 to 91.74 MPa, the elon-
gation of which could reach 2.04%. However, the tensile strength
of DAPU-15 was higher than DAPU-10, but its elongation was
lower than DAPU-10, which might relate to the fact that the ex-
cessive addition of DAdiol may bring a disadvantageous eﬀect
on polyurethane adhesives. To conclude, the formation of an ap-
propriate amount of Diels–Alder covalent bonds would increase
the cross-linking density of polyurethane adhesives and increase
the rigid structure then improve the mechanical strength of
polyurethane adhesives.[29–33] The self-healing behavior of DAPU
might be caused by the dissociation and the recombination of hy-
drogen bonds and the properties of Diels–Alder covalent bonds
of coupling at the low temperature and recombining at the high
temperature. In order to gain insight into the self-healing be-
havior, the mechanical properties of the polyurethane adhesives
with diﬀerent ratios of DAdiol were measured. To quantify the
changes in performances before and after self-healing. The dam-
aged samples were placed in the oven at 130 °C for 2 h to

Our study shows that the molar ratio of the Diels–Alder cova-
lent bonds has a large eﬀect on the reprocessing of the DAPU.
Under thermal stimulation, the dynamic covalent bonds and no-
covalent bonds introduced in this material could form the ther-
mally reversible dynamic networks and could break and recon-
struct that bringing the polyurethane adhesives excellent recycla-
bility, reprocessing ability and largely improving their mechani-
cal properties.
4. Conclusion
In this work, A thermally recyclable and reversible polyurethane
adhesive was designed and synthesized. This polyurethane has
a cross-linked network structure which consists of reversible
dynamic covalent bonds formed by Diels–Alder reaction. The
reversible dynamic cross-linked structure increased the cross-
linking density of the material hence improving the mechanical
strength of the polyurethane adhesives. As a result, the tensile
strength of DAPU-10 could reach 91 MPa. Due to the Diels–Alder
covalent bond, the DAPU shows thermal stimulus properties and
appropriate self-healing function. This work provides a method
to enable polyurethane adhesives with high strength and ther-
mally structure recycling ability which may pave the way for fur-
ther applications of sustainable thermally-responsive self-healing
adhesives.
