performance enabled by partially mixing reversible hard domains

A B S T R A C T  
The reversible chain extender Diels-Alder-diol (DA-diol) containing thermally dissociable Diels-Alder adducts 
(DA adducts) was synthesized and combined with 1,4-butanediol (BDO) as complexed chain extenders used for 
preparing a transparent polyurethane elastomer (DAPU) with high mechanical strength and efficient self-healing 
ability by partially mixing reversible hard domains. The optimal DAPU occupies a desired high tensile strength of 
65 MPa, and its elongation at break and toughness are of 1050 % and 216 MJ⋅m−3, respectively. Meanwhile, the 
DAPU-0.2 elastomer heals at 110 ◦C for 2 h with a healing efficiency of 95 %. The desirable mechanical strength 
and efficient self-healing ability are attributing to the BDO chain extended hard segments ensure the high 
strength at room temperature, while DA-diol chain extended hard segments endow the as-prepared DAPUs with 
self-healing ability at an elevated temperature. This work overall points to a strategy in designing self-healing 
polyurethane elastomers with markedly improved mechanical strength and efficient self-healing ability by 
partially mixing reversible hard domains.   
1. Introduction 
Inspired from nature, self-healing materials, capable of repairing 
physical damages and restoring original performances under suitable 
stimulus, have been developed and made significant advances over the 
last two decades. Self-healing elastomers are one of the most studied 
materials since they have broad and promising applications, including in 
flexible sensors, [1,2] artificial skins, [3] energy conversion devices, 
[4–6] lithium-ion batteries, [7–9] responsive robotics [10,11] and pro­
tective coatings. [12,13] However, the mechanical strength and tough­
ness required by load-bearing applications and self-healing ability 
enabled by the bond reversibility and chain flexibility are mutually 
exclusive in most structure design of healable elastomers. To prepare 
high performance self-healing materials, several strategies are taken into 
account, such as to generate a high density of units without crystalli­
zation and covalent bonds, [14,15] to construct reversible covalent 
crosslinking by dissociative dynamic bonds [16,17] and to achieve a 
well-controlled balance between hierarchical physical crosslinking and 
microphase separation [18,19] in the materials. 
Thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) elastomers with typically alter­
nating hard and soft segments are prone to form microphase separation 
morphology with glassy or crystalline hard domains dispersed in the soft 
matrix. [20–22] The hard domains usually act as fillers and physical 
crosslinking to enhance the mechanical strength. Suitable microphase 
separation degree and ratio of hard domains and soft matrixes give ac­
cess to an elastomeric TPU material with excellent mechanical proper­
ties. It is foreseeable that the high-performance TPU materials generally 
have high-melting crystalline hard domains, high molecular weight and 
inter-chain entanglement. Thus, the diffusion rate of molecular chains is 
slow that results in the incapable of self-healing ability. To endow TPU 
with self-healing ability, non-covalent interactions including hydrogen 
bonds, [23–26] coordination bonds, [27,28] and so on, and different 
reversible bonds such as disulfide bonds, [29–32] imine bonds, [33,34] 
phenol-carbamate bonds, [35] urea bonds, [36] and so on, are intro­
duced into TPU backbones as chain extender or crosslinker that results 
in the efficient self-healing ability of TPU. Multiple hydrogen bonds 
introduced play a dual role in enhancing and promoting self-healing. 
The breaking and recombination of dynamic hydrogen bonds confer 
self-healing ability and mechanical enhancement properties of elasto­
mers on the molecular scale. [23–25] By utilizing the rigidity and high 
dynamism of the C = N double bond of imine, and controlling the degree 
of microphase separation by adjusting the content of hard segments,

polyurethane elastomers with high mechanical strength and excellent 
self-healing performance were prepared. [33,34] However, due to the 
weak nature of reversible bonds and improved chain mobility, intrinsic 
self-healing elastomers have compromised mechanical properties. 
[37–39] It is critical to achieve a balance between strong mechanical 
properties as traditional TPU and satisfying self-healing performances 
by a simple and efficient way. 
Diels-Alder (DA), first reported by Otto Diels and Kurt Alder, [40] is 
one of the widest used reversible reactions and occupies in develop self- 
healing materials with high modulus and high strength. [41–43] This 
dynamic covalent bond has gained a lot of research interest in the last 
few decades due to the highly sensitive temperature-dependent revers­
ibility. Formed by the cyclo-addition reaction of diene and dienophile, 
DA adducts are stable in a relatively low temperature while reverse re­
action (rDA) trend to happen with increasing temperature. [44,45] As 
the most commonly reactants, furan derivatives and maleimide de­
rivatives have a high reaction rate under a mild condition with high 
conversion rate and a relatively low reverse reaction rate at above 90 ◦C. 
[46,47] Thus, DA bond is an excellent candidate for constructing self- 
healing materials in a simple and efficient way under a mild condi­
tion. And more, the reactants are widely supplied in raw material, cheap 
and easy to synthesize which endow practicable industrial application of 
DA bond. 
Herein, to achieve a balance between mechanical properties and self- 
healing ability, an appropriate dynamic chain extender ratio was 
needed. In this work, the thermally dissociable DA-diol as reversible 
chain extender was synthesized. Combining with BDO, the complexed 
chain extenders give access to a high strength self-healing polyurethane 
elastomer. On the one hand, due to the BDO chain extended hard do­
mains, the polyurethane elastomer exhibits excellent strength at room 
temperature. On the other hand, because of the temperature sensitivity 
of DA reaction equilibrium, DA-diol chain extended hard segments 
endow the polyurethane elastomer with self-healing ability at an 
elevated temperature. Through tuning the dynamic chain extender ratio, 
the varied self-healing ability and mechanical properties were exam­
ined. By partially incorporating DA-diol chain extender in a ratio of 0.2 
and 0.3 to BDO chain extender, the DAPUs ensure an efficient self- 
healing ability with little sacrifice of its mechanical strength. The 
optimal DAPU-0.2 occupies a desirable high tensile strength of 65 MPa, 
and its elongation at break and toughness are of 1050 % and 216 
MJ⋅m−3, respectively. Moreover, the DAPU elastomer achieves a healing 
efficiency of 95.0 % in 2 h at 110 ◦C. This work presents a facile strategy 
to achieve a balance of desirable mechanical properties and satisfying 
self-healing performances for traditional PU elastomers by partially 
mixing reversible hard domains.

2.1. Materials 
Polytetramethylene ether glycol (PTMEG, Mn = 1000 g⋅mol−1) was 
supplied from BASF (Ludwigshafen, Germany). Dicyclohexylmethane 
4,4′-diisocyanate (HMDI, trans, trans-isomer) was supplied by Wanhua

dimethyl ether and 1,4-butanendiol (BDO) were purchased from 
Guangzhou Chemical Reagent (Guangzhou, China). N, N-dimethyl 
formamide (DMF, extra dry, with molecular sieves, water content ≤50 
ppm) was purchased from Energy Chemical (Shanghai, China).

2.3. Synthesis of DAPU elastomers 
A series of DAPU elastomers with different DA-diol chain extending 
ratio were prepared through a two-step polymerization method, as 
illustrated in Scheme 2. The detailed formulations of all DAPUs were 
displayed in Table 1. Taken DAPU-0.2 as example, the detailed synthesis 
procedures were described as below. In a round-bottom flask, PTMEG 
(40 g, 40 mmol) were dehydrated at 120 ◦C under vacuum for 2 h. After 
cooling to 70 ◦C, HMDI (22.0 g, 84 mmol) and DBTDL (0.6 mg) were 
added and stirred for about 30 min under the protection of N2. Then, 
anhydrous DMF (100 g), BDO (2.88 g, 36 mmol) and DBTDL (0.08 g) 
were added and stirred for 20 min at 70 ◦C. Afterwards, DA-diol (1.91 g, 
4 mmol) and DBTDL (0.08 g) were added and continued to react at 70 ◦C 
for 4 h. Finally, after dilution of the polymer solution with DMF to solid 
concentration of 20 wt% to reduce the viscosity, it was poured into the 
PTFE mold and dried in the oven at 70 ◦C for 12 h. After vacuum drying 
at 70 ◦C for at least 48 h, a yellowish transparent elastomer film was 
obtained. The 1H NMR of different DAPUs are showed in Figure S5.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Materials design and characterization 
DAPU elastomers with different DA-diol chain extended hard seg­
ments were prepared through a two-step polymerization method, as 
illustrated in Scheme 2. First, HMDI and PTMEG were prepolymerized to 
obtain NCO terminated prepolymer. Then, the prepolymer was chain 
extended with BDO and DA-diol successively with different ratios, fol­
lowed by the solvent evaporation in the Teflon mold to give transparent 
elastomer films. In order to investigate the independence influence of 
DA-diol chain extending ratio on the performance of DAPUs, the effects 
of NCO: OH prepolymerization ratio and hard segment content should 
be suppressed by controlling these two variables. Due to the molecular 
mass differences of DA-diol and BDO chain extenders, it was impossible 
to keep these two variables unchanged simultaneously, while varying 
the DA-diol chain extending ratio. Thus, on the basis of total NCO: OH =
1.05 instead of the stoichiometric NCO: OH = 1 (Considering the inev­
itable reaction between water vapor and NCO– group during the reac­
tion process, as well as the influence of the secondary reaction between 
urethane and NCO– groups), these two variables were both varied 
slightly to balance and reduce their effects. The first NCO: OH pre­
polymerization ratios were set 2.1: 1 to 2.0:1 thus controlled the hard 
segment contents were at about 40 wt% in the formulation. The detailed 
reaction formulas with different ratio of BDO: DA-diol were listed in 
Table 1. 
All the DAPU films were of highly transparent elastic with light 
yellow color. The color gradually became deeper with the increasing DA 
adduct content. Fig. 1a showed the typical appearance and the UV–vis 
transmittance spectra of DAPU films. The adsorptions of DAPU films on 
visible light were mainly in the blue light region, originated from the 
imide conjugated structure in the DA adduct. And the rising adsorptions 
in the 300–500 nm blue light region were consistent with the increased 
DA adduct contents. In the non-blue visible region above 500 nm, all 
DAPUs, except the DAPU-1.0 with the highest content of DA adduct, 
have a very high transparency with a transmittance greater than 88 %. 
The ATR-FTIR spectra of different DAPUs were presented in Fig. 1b. It 
was found that all the spectra were all similar, except the weak peak 
band centered at 1775 cm−1. This peak is the characteristic adsorption 
peak of DA adduct, and even though it is quite weak, the trend of 
increased peak heights is consistent with the increasing DA-diol ratios of 
DAPUs. 
For further check the chemical structure of the prepared DAPUs, they 
were dissolved in CDCl3, and characterized by 1H NMR. The results were 
showed in Figure S5. The peaks at 6.54 and 6.48 ppm are the charac­
teristic of two hydrogens a and a′ on the carbon–carbon double bond on 
the six-membered ring of the DA adduct, while the peaks at 5.28 ppm 
and 3.76 ppm belong to the hydrogen b on the DA adduct and the 
hydrogen d on the HMDI, respectively. The ratio of the integral value of 
the peaks mentioned above was matched to the DA-diol chain extending 
ratio of DAPUs, which indicated that the DA-diol was successfully 
introduced into the DAPUs according to the setting ratio. The molecular 
weight of each DAPU was characterized by GPC shown as Fig. 2 and 
Table 2. The molecular weight of DAPU-0.0 only extended by BDO was 
the largest, while the DAPU-1.0 that only using DA-diol as the chain 
extender exhibited the smallest molecular weight. Overall, the molar 
mass of DAPUs decreased inconspicuously with the increasing DA-diol 
chain extender ratio. This result indicated that the molecular weight 
of DAPUs was not almost imparted by the DA reversible adduct at room 
temperature because of its large equilibrium constant. And thus, the low 
molecular weight would not affect the performances of DAPUs at room 
temperature.

DSC was used to analyze DAPUs with different DA adduct contents, 
and the DSC curves of the first heating scan of each DAPU are shown in 
Figure S6 and the data are listed in Table S1. As the proportion of DA- 
diol chain extending ratio increases, the soft segment Tg of DAPU 
gradually increases, indicating better compatibility obtained between 
the hard and soft segments of DA-diol chain extender. The introduction 
of DA-diol chain extender would gradually reduce the degree of 
microphase separation of DAPUs. For DAPU-0.0 chain extended 
completely by BDO, there are two endothermic peaks at between 70 and 
120◦C, belonging to ordered structures or microcrystals with different 
degree of order formed by hard chain segments of different lengths, and 
the endothermic peaks that consume heat when heated and destroyed. 
When introducing a portion of DA diol chain extenders, that is, partially 
mixing reversible domains, due to the dynamic reversibility of DA ad­
ducts, different molecular chains could be dynamically reorganized, 
resulting in a more uniform order of the ordered structure of hard 
segment stacking in various parts of DAPUs. This leads to the two 
endothermic peaks transferred into one as shown in Figure S6. This 
endothermic peak shifts slightly towards high temperature with the 
increasing DA diol chain extender ratio, mainly due to the increased 
mobility of the molecular chains, and thus leading to a slight increase in 
the ordering degree of hard segments with some extent. However, the 
increasing DA-diol chain extender ratio would reduce the degree of 
microphase separation of DAPUs as well as decrease the proportion of 
hard segments that formed ordered structure, as shown in the thermal 
effect of this endothermic peak Δ Hm gradually decreases in Table S1. 
When the chain extending ratio of DA diol is more than 50 %, the ab­
sorption peak has even decreased to complete disappear. The absorption 
peak between 100 and 160 ◦C in Figure S6 is the reaction heat absorbed 
by the DA reversible adducts rDA decomposition with a peak tempera­
ture of around 140 ◦C. The enthalpy of this absorption peak Δ HrDA 
enhances with the increasing DA-diol chain extending ratio, which is 
consistent with expectations.

3.3. Mechanical and self-healing properties of DAPUs 
In order to investigate the effect of the increasing chain extender 
ratio of DA-diol on the mechanical properties of DAPUs, the mechanical 
properties with different DA-diol chain extender ratio were examined by 
tensile tests. The typical stress–strain curves obtained are shown in Fig. 5 
and the mechanical performance data are listed in Table S2. The overall 
DAPUs exhibit a trend of decreasing Young’s modulus, tensile strength, 
and increasing elongation at break with the increasing DA-diol chain 
extending ratio. The main reason is that due to the poor structural 
regularity of DA-diol and the presence of two stereoisomers, the hard 
segments of DA-diol chain extension are difficult to arrange into ordered 
structure, resulting in the ordered micro regions of the original BDO 
chain extension hard segments in DAPU being gradually destroyed by 
the hard segments of DA-diol chain extension. This reduces the degree of 
microphase separation of DAPU, and thus reduces its tensile strength, 
but increases its elongation. When the chain extending ratio of DA-diol is 
low, that is, the ordered micro regions of BDO hard segments in DAPU- 
0.1 to DAPU-0.3 have not been completely destroyed, and still play a 
role in filler reinforcement and physical crosslinking, consequently 
maintain relatively high mechanical strength. When the chain extending 
ratio of DA-diol is greater than 50 %, the ordered micro regions of hard 
segments are basically destroyed, and thus the degree of microphase 
separation significantly decreases as well as the strength sharply de­
creases, that the poor tensile strength and higher elongation at break of 
DAPU-0.5 and DAPU-1.0 shown in Fig. 5a. 
In order to investigate the elastic recovery performance and fatigue 
resistance of DAPUs, a typical DAPU-0.2 was subjected to cyclic tensile 
tests. The stress–strain curve of the DAPU-0.2 during 10 cycles of cyclic 
loading tensile tests in Fig. 5b. Fig. 5c shows the calculated elastic re­
covery rate and hysteresis area for each cycle based on Fig. 5b. The 
elasticity of the DAPU-0.2 is of an elastic recovery rate of 94 % in the 
first cycle, and nearly 100 % in each subsequent cycle. Moreover, after 
10 cyclic stretching at 500 % strain, the tensile strength was up to 60 
MPa from Fig. 5b. From the energy loss of the hysteresis loop in each 
cycle, except the maximum energy loss in the first hysteresis loop, the 
energy loss of the hysteresis loop in the subsequent loading cycles 
gradually approaches an equilibrium value. The main reason is that the 
energy loss in the first loading cycle mainly includes the enthalpy energy 
consumed by hydrogen bond disconnection, while the energy loss in the 
subsequent loading cycles mainly consists of the entropy energy released 
by the rebound of soft segment molecular chains. The above indicates 
that when the DA-diol chain extending ratio is not high, the hard 
segment micro region formed by the separation of hard and soft seg­
ments plays a typical physical cross-linking role, leading to excellent 
elastic and mechanical properties. And more, the stability of the hard 
segment micro region is strong, leading to good fatigue resistance.

The above results indicate that the large equilibrium shift in the 
reverse reaction of DA adducts is very conducive to the self-healing 
process. By introducing a small amount of DA reversible adducts into 
the long chain of polymer molecules, the reversible equilibrium of DA 
adducts can greatly weaken the hindrance effect of molecular chain 
entanglement and microphase separation on molecular chain diffusion 
without significantly reducing mechanical strength. Thus, it enables the 
molecular chains to rapidly diffuse with the assistance of DA reversible 
equilibrium reaction, and then fill and narrow the scratch gap until the 
scratch damage is completely repaired. The higher the chain extension 
ratio of DA-diol used, the higher the proportion of DA adducts in the 
molecular chain presented as well as the weakened degree of micro­
phase separation. The combined effect leads to a rapid improvement in 
the self-healing performance of DAPUs with an increasing chain 
extending ratio of DA-diol. 
In order to further quantitively evaluate the self-healing efficiencies 
of DAPUs, tensile tests of the DAPU films healed at different tempera­
tures were conducted. Fig. 7 showed the stress–strain curves of DAPUs at 
varied healing temperature and the exact data are listed in Table S3. It is 
obvious that the incorporation of DA-diol significantly improved the 
self-healing efficiencies of the DAPU films. Without the dynamic chain 
extender, the healing efficiency of DAPU-0.0 that healed at 120 ◦C 
within 4 h was of 36.9 %, while the healing efficiency of DAPU-0.1 was 
of 86.9 % in 4 h at 110 ◦C and DAPU-0.2 was of 81.6 % in 1 h at 110 ◦C. 
DAPU-0.3 occupies a higher healing efficiency of 87.0 % at 100 ◦C 
within 1 h. As expected, DAPU-0.5 wholly healed at 80 ◦C and even the 
temperature of 60 ◦C was an efficient temperature for DAPU-1.0. 
Meanwhile, it is worth noting that compared with DAPU-0.0 chain 
extended by BDO completely, all the DAPUs achieve a certain healing 
degree at a lower temperature of 60–80 ◦C due to the DA reversible 
reaction would proceed at 60–80 ◦C and the rearrangement of the mo­
lecular chain happen in such condition. For example, DAPU-0.2 achieves 
a healing efficiency of 48.9 % at 80 ◦C for 4 h, and DAPU-0.3 achieves a 
healing efficiency of 65.9 % under the same conditions. The reason for 
this phenomena is that even there is a large forward reaction equilib­
rium constant at 60–80 ◦C, however, due to the high reaction rates of 
both the forward and reverse reactions present at this time, the DA ad­
ducts on different molecular chains are still continuously recombining. 
As long as the reverse reaction rate is high, the DAPUs still show a 
certain degree of self-healing ability, and resulted in a satisfying healing 
efficiency of DAPU-0.1 to DAPU-1.0 contrast to DAPU-0.0.

3.4. Rheology and self-healing mechanism of DAPUs 
To explain the self-healing mechanism of DAPU enabled by partially 
mixing reversible hard domains of combination of DA-diol and BDO 
chain extenders, rheological frequency sweep tests at 100 ◦C were 
conducted as shown in Fig. 8. The relaxation time τ, known as the in­
verse of the crossover frequency of storage modulus G′ and loss modulus 
G″, is closely related with the chain mobility which is critical to the 
process of self-healing. The relaxation time of DAPU-0.1 to DAPU-0.5 
are 11.9 s, 1.04 s, 0.207 s, and 0.073 s which is steeply decreased 
when the DA-diol chain extending ratio raising. The efficient self- 
healing ability in DAPU films was attributed to the chain mobility 
improved signally by incorporating the DA-diol chain extender. Zero 
shear viscosity (η0) is a direct indicator for the self-healing process. As 
shown in Fig. 8b, when the frequency extended to infinitely small, the 
complex viscosities for DAPU-0.1 to DAPU-1.0 tended to a constant 
called zero shear viscosity. On the contrary, the zero shear viscosity of 
DAPU-0.0 approached infinity. It indicated that the zero shear viscosity 
decreased distinctly in consequence of the increasing DA-diol chain 
extender ratio and resulted in the higher self-healing efficiency. 
4. Conclusions 
In summary, a super strong DAPU elastomer with efficient self- 
healing performance enabled by partially mixing reversible DA bonds 
was successfully synthesized. Incorporating a small quantity of DA-diol 
in hard domains exhibits desirable mechanical properties under ambient 
temperature as well as efficient self-healing ability at an elevated 
temperature. When the DA-diol chain extender ratio is of 0.2 and 0.3, 
the maximum tensile strength of DAPUs is over 65 MPa and the elon­
gation at break is up to 1053 % with the toughness of 216 MJ⋅m−3. 
Meanwhile, the self-healing process were completely in 2 h at 110 ◦C 
with the self-healing degree of 95 %. A small amount of DA reversible 
adduct resulted in a slight increase in the microphase separation degree 
in DAPUs which improved chain mobility and gave access to a steeply 
increased of self-healing efficiency. This work provided a facile micro­
phase separation strategy by designed reversible hard domain for self- 
healing robust polyurethane materials.
