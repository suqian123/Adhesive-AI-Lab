Synthesis and characterization of self-healing cross-linked non-isocyanate
polyurethanes based on Diels-Alder reaction with unsaturated polyester

Key Laboratory of Carbon Fiber and Functional Polymers (Beijing University of Chemical Technology), Ministry of Education, College of Materials Science and Engineering,

A B S T R A C T
A green method is established to prepare soft self-healing cross-linked non-isocyanate polyurethanes (SH-
NIPUs). Three non-isocyanate polyurethane prepolymers with furan terminal groups (F-NIPUs) were synthesized
through a ring-opening reaction of diglycerol bis(cyclic carbonate) with isophoronediamine and furfurylamine
under mild reaction conditions. New SH-NIPUs were prepared via a Diels–Alder reaction between F-NIPUs and
unsaturated polyester. The obtained SH-NIPUs were characterized by 1H NMR, ATI-FTIR, DSC, polarized optical
microscope, and tensile strength after being damaged and healed. The SH-NIPUs exhibited a Tg between -4 and
20 °C, and a relatively low reverse Diels–Alder temperature between 90 and 100 °C. Soft SH-NIPUs with high self-
healing efficiency up to 100% were achieved successfully.
1. Introduction
Polymer materials are widely used in our daily life. In their appli-
cations, micro-cracks or damages are inevitably generated when they
suffer from heat, mechanical attack, or chemical erosion. The cracks are
usually deep inside and difficult to be detected. They cause mechanical
property reduced and service lifetime shortened. Inspired by the self-
healing phenomenon of organism, scientists develop a series of poly-
mers with self-healing property. Many methods have been adopted to
prepare self-healing polymers [1]. The self-healing strategies used in-
clude introducing reversible covalent bonds [2,3], hydrogen bonding
[4], coordination [5], and reactive microcapsules [6].
Polyurethane (PU) is one of important polymers. PUs are widely
used as foams, elastomers, coatings, adhesives, and sealants due to their
excellent mechanical property [7]. Self-healing PUs (SH-PUs) have also
been investigated in recent decade. Furan-maleimide Diels-Alder (DA)
reaction becomes an effective way to synthesize thermo-reversible PUs
[8–10].
Current DA SH-PUs are mainly synthesized through isocyanate
route according to three major methods. The most used method is to
introduce furan terminal or pendent groups into PU prepolymers, and
then react on bismaleimides (BMIs) [10–13] or multi-maleimide com-
pounds [8] to synthesize SH-PUs. The second method is to prepare PU
prepolymers containing maleimide pendant groups, and react on di-
functional furan compounds to form SH-PUs [14,15]. The third method
is to synthesize SH-PUs directly by reacting the preformed HO-termi-
nated DA adducts with diisocyanates [9]. In the literatures, BMIs or
maleimide-containing compounds are usually synthesized through
several steps of solution reactions, and the yield is commonly lower
than 58% [8,9].
The purpose of this paper is to establish a green and effective way to
synthesize self-healing crosslinked non-isocyanate polyurethanes (SH-
NIPUs). In this paper, three PU prepolymers containing furan terminal
groups (F-NIPUs) were prepared through a non-isocyanate method. An
unsaturated polyester (UP) was prepared from melt polycondensation
of maleic anhydride (MA), adipic acid (AA) and butanediol (BD). Then,
SH-NIPUs were prepared by reacting F-NIPUs with UP. UP replaces
BMIs or maleimide-containing compounds to conduct DA addition with
furan groups. The electron-withdrawing carbonyl groups in UP make
C = C bonds reactive and easy to conduct DA reaction with furan
groups.

2.2. Synthesis of diglycerol bis(cyclic carbonate) (DGDC)
Transesterification reaction was carried out analogously to the lit-
erature [16]. In a 250 mL three-neck flask equipped with a magnetic
stirrer and a reflux condenser, the mixture of 50 g (0.30 mol) DG,
160.5 g (1.78 mol) DMC, and 0.25 g (1.80 mmol) K2CO3 was heated
under stirring at 70 °C for 24 h. After evaporation of the excessive DMC
and the formed methanol, a liquid crude product was obtained. It was
crystallized in ethyl acetate at 5 °C for 24 h. White solid DGDC with
79% yield was obtained (Scheme 1). The structure of DGDC was con-
firmed by FT-IR (Fig. S1) and 1H NMR (Fig. S2) spectra.
2.3. Synthesis of F-NIPUs
FA, IPDA and DGDC were placed at different n values (Table 1) in
100 mL three-necked flasks. The bulk polymerization was conducted at
70 °C for 8 h to obtain F-NIPUs (Scheme 2).
2.4. Synthesis of UP
MA (0.08 mol, 7.84 g), AA (0.02 mol, 2.92 g), BD (0.13 mol, 11.7 g),
P-TSA (0.2 wt%, 0.045 g), SnCl2 (0.2 wt%, 0.045 g) and MEHQ (0.5 wt
%, 0.112 g) were placed in a 100 mL three-necked flask. Under ni-
trogen, the reaction temperature was gradually raised to 180 °C and
held for 2 h under atmospheric pressure. The reaction was continued at
1300 Pa for another 2 h. An UP liquid was obtained at a yield of 85%
(Scheme 3). The acid value (Av) of UP was 3.2 mgKOH∙g-1, and the
hydroxyl value (Qv) was 105.2 mgKOH∙g-1.
2.5. Synthesis of SH-NIPUs
UP and F-NIPU-1, F-NIPU-2, or F-NIPU-3 were added into different
50 mL beakers at a 1:1 molar ratio of the MA units in UP to the furan
groups in F-NIPUs. The mixtures were stirred at 150 °C for 3 min to
uniformity, and were poured into dumbbell tetrafluoroethylene molds.
The samples were transferred into a vacuum oven at 150 °C. Bubbles
were removed under a pressure of 1300 Pa for 3 min. Then, the samples
were cured at 60 °C for 24 h to solid state (Table 2).
2.6. Characterization methods
The Av and Qv of UP were detected according to the procedure
described in the literature [17]. One gram of UP was dissolved in 15 ml
DMF and titrated with 0.05 N NaOH ethanol solution to detect Av. The
determining method of the H2N- content in F-NIPUs was the same as
that of Qv. The Mn and dispersity (D) of F-NIPUs were characterized on
Waters 150C size exclusion chromatography (SEC) at 25 °C with tetra-
hydrofuran as eluent (1 mL/min). The 1H NMR spectra of samples were
recorded on a Bruker NMR Spectrometer using DMSO-d6 as solvent. The
chemical shifts were reported as parts per million downfield from tet-
ramethylsilane. ATI-FTIR spectra were performed on a Paragon 1000
spectrometer (Perkin-Elmer) in the range of 4000 to 400 cm-1. The
differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) scans of SH-NIPUs were ex-
amined on a TA Instruments Q2000 under nitrogen atmosphere at a
heating rate of 10 °C min-1. About 5-10 mg samples were used for the
measurements.
After SH-NIPUs were prepared in Section 2.5, they were cooled and
kept in a desiccator at room temperature. Then, the swelling property of
them were determined soon. The swelling property of samples was
characterized by swelling them in DMF at 50 °C for 12 h. The un-
dissolved parts were filtrated, washed with ethanol, and dried in va-
cuum oven at 50 °C for 24 h. The dissolution ratio was calculated from
the dissolution portion of samples.
The cracks on polymer films were observed on a polarizing optical
microscopy (LEICA DM LP) equipped with a temperature programmed
heating stage (TMS 94). Then, the self-healing property of SH-NIPUs
was evaluated.
The mechanical properties of the original and the healed samples
were measured on an Instron 4465 testing machine. Elongation rate

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Synthesis of F-NIPUs
Within the margin of error, complete conversion of DGDC, FA, and
IPDA to F-NIPUs was confirmed by FTIR. The absorption peaks of vO-H
(3325 cm-1), carbamate vC = O (1694 cm-1), and secondary amide δN-
H (1533 cm-1) proved successful ring-opening reaction between DGDC
and FA or IPDA (Scheme 2). The δC-H absorption of furan rings
(740 cm-1) demonstrates the retention of furan groups in F-NIPUs
(Fig. 1 and Fig. S3).
According to the measurement of amine values (Table 1), the con-
tent of the H2N- terminal groups in F-NIPUs was very low. It indicates
that the reaction in Scheme 2 was nearly completed.
3.2. Synthesis of UP
FT-IR confirmed that MA, AA, and BD were completely converted to
UP (Scheme 3). The vO-H peak (3200-3700 cm-1) of –COOH and –OH
terminal groups became negligible. The ester vC = O peak (1717 cm-1)
appeared, and the vC = C peak (1642 cm-1) remained (Fig. 2). They all
prove successful esterification and polycondensation of MA, AA, and
BA.

3.3. Synthesis of SH-NIPUs
It was confirmed that F-NIPU-1 and UP were converted to SH-NIPU-
1. First, a shoulder peak specific to the ester vC = O of DA adduct ap-
peared at 1734 cm-1(Fig. 3). Second, the δC-H absorption peak of furan
rings decreased at 740 cm-1, and an out-of-plane δC-H vibration peak in
DA adducts appeared at 777 cm-1. These two characteristic peaks
(1734 cm-1 and 777 cm-1) of DA adduct were assigned based on the
reference [18], in which furan-dimethyl fumarate DA adduct was pre-
pared and characterized by FT-IR spectrum. These peaks indicated that
SH-NIPUs were successful synthesized from UP and F-NIPUs via the DA
reaction. Fig. S4 shows the deconvolution curves of the δC-H vibration
peaks in DA adducts and furan rings. The DA conversion of SH-NIPU-1
was calculated according to the deconvolution analysis. It was about
25%.
The peaks at 6.85 and 6.25 ppm in Fig. 4c demonstrate that the
C = C groups of UP are in trans- and cis- structure (hydrogens 1 and 4
in Fig. 5), and the latter is the major structure. The peaks at 4.17, 6.21,
6.38 and 7.64 ppm in Fig. 4b demonstrate the presence of the furan
rings (hydrogens 12, 11, 10, and 9 in Fig. 5) in F-NIPU-1. The char-
acteristic peaks of the DA bond at 2.80, 3.10 and 5.11 ppm in Fig. 4a
demonstrate the occurrence of the DA addition (hydrogens 14, 15, and
16 in Fig. 5). The peak at 6.77 ppm in Fig. 4a arises from the hydrogens
17 in cis- and trans- DA adducts (Fig. 5), while the peak at 6.46 ppm
corresponds to the hydrogen 18 in trans- DA adduct. These hydrogens
from 14 to 18 were assigned based on the 1H NMR spectra of furan-
dimethyl fumarate DA adduct and furan-dimethyl maleate DA adduct in
the reference [18].
3.4. Swelling property of SH-NIPUs
To prove the prepared SH-NIPUs are crosslinked polyurethanes, we
immersed them in DMF at 50 °C for 12 h, and recorded the mass of
samples before and after soaking.
In Fig. 6, the left and right pictures of each SH-NIPU correspond to
the sample before and after soaking. After soaking, SH-NIPUs all
swelled, but none of them was dissolved completely. Table 2 shows that
the dissolution ratios of SH-NIPUs are relatively high after soaking. This
indicates that SH-NIPUs are partially cross-linked polyurethanes. With
the increase of n in F-NIPUs (Table 1), the dissolution ratio shows an
upward trend, indicating that the crosslinking degree decreased. In-
creasing n causes the molecular weight of F-NIPUs increased and the
furan content decreased. Thus, the DA crosslinking density in SH-NIPUs
decreased, with the dissolution ratio increased.

Table 2). Their Tg is -6, -4, and 20 °C, respectively. Increasing n means
the F-NIPUs have higher molecular weight and higher rigid IPDA

Meanwhile, an endothermic peak appears in the range from 90 to
100 °C. It corresponds to the temperature of reverse DA (rDA) reaction.
The peak intensity is related to the amount of heat absorption in rDA
reaction. With the increase of the n in F-NIPUs, the endothermic peak
becomes weak, because a high n in F-NIPUs lowers the DA crosslinking
density in SH-NIPUs. The rDA temperature of SH-NIPUs is obviously
lower than that of furan-maleimide DA systems, in which the rDA
temperature is at about 130 °C [8]. The reason is that UP is a polymer
with opening chains. The maleic and fumaric ester groups in UP have
higher steric hindrance than the closed maleimide rings in BMIs or
other maleimide-containing compounds, leading to the decrease of the
rDA temperature in UP-derived SH-NIPUs. A similar DA adduct was
reported by Wei et al. They studied a fulvene- dichloromaleate DA
system, in which the fulvene group is similar to furan ring, while the
dichloromaleate group has higher steric hindrance than maleate and
furamate groups. Higher steric hindrance makes the rDA reaction of
fulvene-dichloromaleate DA system takes place at merely 37 °C [19].
3.6. Polarizing optical microscopy (POM) characterization of self-healing
performance
The self-healing property of SH-NIPU-1 was investigated by obser-
ving crack evolution under POM equipped with a heating stage. On a
0.8 × 0.8 mm SH-NIPU-1 splint, we drew a deep crack in the lateral
direction and a shallow crack in the oblique direction. Fig. 8a was the
starting state. When the splint was held at 120 °C for 1 min, the lateral
crack was significantly narrowed (Fig. 8b), and most of it was repaired.
At 2 min, two bubbles appeared at the intersection of the cracks
(Fig. 8c). Maybe some air at the cutting edges was entangled when the
molecular segments moved, and two bubbles were formed in repairing
process. After 3 min, the oblique crack was basically completely re-
paired (Fig. 8d). Only the surface part was not repaired. At 5 min, the
oblique crack was completely repaired, and the lateral crack still re-
mained at surface layer (Fig. 8e). After 8 min, the lateral and oblique
cracks were all completely repaired (Fig. 8f). In a summary, SH-NIPUs
achieve good self-repair of cracks at 120 °C in several minutes. Due to
slightly rough surface, light reflection caused some irregular white
areas were observed in some photos.
During repairing at 120 °C, SH-NIPU-1 kept solid state at least in
30 min. Dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA) (Fig. S5) shows that SH-
NIPU-1 exhibits the storage modulus (E’) of 3.5 MPa at 85 °C. At this
temperature, SH-NIPU-1 was solid with certain tensile strength. Beyond
85 °C, the splints were pulled apart because they became more soft and
weak. Then, the detection did not continue. Fig. S6 shows the repairing
process of SH-NIPU-1 at 85 °C. At this temperature, most of the cutting
edges were repaired in 1 h. However, cutting traces still remained until
3 h. Repairing at 85 °C is much slower than that at 120 °C because of
low repairing temperature and insufficient dissociation of DA adducts.
On Fig. 8s and Fig. S6, some small or tiny black dots were found due
to birefringence. In UP preparation, the fumarate or maleate content in
UP was heavily influenced by the reaction temperature and time.
Temperature fluctuation in melt polycondensation might result in some
UP molecules with relatively high fumarate content. These UP mole-
cules exhibited relatively high melting point and poor solubility. When
UP was mixed with F-NIPUs, some tiny crystals were formed, and part
of them were left in SH-NIPUs due to incomplete DA reaction. In the
observation of SH-NIPU-1 under POM, the tiny crystals caused light
extinction. Thus, some tiny black dots were observed.
3.7. Mechanical properties and self-healing efficiency
The self-healing efficiency of SH-NIPUs was obtained by tensile test.
When a dumbbell-shaped splint was cut from middle, the cut portions
were then placed in a dumbbell-shaped mold, heated at 120 °C for
30 min, and placed in an oven at 60 °C for 24 h. A self-healed SH-NIPU
splint was obtained. The tensile test of SH-NIPUs before and after self-
healing was conducted.

\n--- PAGE 6 ---\n4. Conclusions
Self-healing partially cross-linked non-isocyanate polyurethanes, i.e.
SH-NIPUs, were successfully synthesized via a DA addition reaction
between F-NIPUs and UP. The cross-linking was verified by swelling
analysis. DA bonds make SH-NIPUs show thermal reversibility. DSC and
POM characterizations demonstrate the self-healing properties of SH-
NIPUs. Cracks on SH-NIPU surface disappear within 8 min at 120 °C.
During repairing at 120 °C, SH-NIPU-1 kept solid state at least in
30 min. At 85 °C, SH-NIPU-1 was in solid state with certain tensile
strength, and most of the cutting edges were repaired in 1 h. The me-
chanical properties were affected by the molecular weight and the IDU
content of F-NIPUs. Higher n and more IDU units introduced lead to the
increase of tensile strength. SH-NIPUs show great self-healing efficiency
and excellent elastic recovery. Increasing IDU units increases the steric
hindrance of SH-NIPUs, leading to the self-healing efficiency and elastic
recovery all decreased. SH-NIPUs are soft and rubbery non-isocyanate
cross-linked
polyurethanes
prepared
through
a
green
method.
Compared with traditional DA reaction from BMIs or maleimide-con-
taining compounds, UP based DA reaction exhibits obviously lower rDA
temperature. This advantage may improve the repairing speed and ef-
ficiency, and make the applications of self-healing PUs more con-
venient.
