A B S T R A C T  
Great progress has been made in the research of self-healing materials in recent decades, however, there are still 
great challenges in achieving high mechanical strength and wide scratch healing simultaneously. Herein, a novel 
polyurethane elastomer (PUDA) containing polycaprolactone (PCL) soft segments and Diels-Alder (DA) 
adducting structure was specially designed and synthesized. The experimental results show that the PUDA 
elastomer after being trained not only had ultrahigh mechanical strength, but also had satisfactory ability in 
healing of wide scratches. It was found that the elastomer displayed a high tensile strength up to 32.88 MPa, 
which is unprecedented when compared to those reported self-healing elastomers. The PUDA elastomer material 
also had excellent healing ability towards wide scratches: an 80-μm-wide scratch could be completely healed 
after heating at 110◦C for 6 min. Furthermore, the elastomer material could also attain more than 100 % re­
covery of mechanical strength, with its recovered tensile strength reaching up to 34.58 MPa. The developed 
PUDA elastomer demonstrates quite promising perspective in material science and engineering field, especially 
for the protection of surface coating.   
1. Introduction 
As one of the most important smart materials, self-healing materials 
have been developed very rapidly in the past few decades [1–11]. 
Generally, self-healing materials can be classified into two main groups: 
extrinsic type and intrinsic type. Although the former has been studied 
for a long period of time, the irreversibility still remains as an 
outstanding issue and greatly hinders its practical application. For this 
reason, the latter — intrinsic self-healing materials — has attracted more 
and more concerns from both academia and industries in recent years. 
Particularly, self-healing materials based on dynamic reversible bonds, 
such as hydrogen bonds [12–16], disulfide bonds [17–20] and 
Diels-Alder (DA) bonds [21–24], have become the focus of research. 
So far, tremendous efforts have been devoted to reduce the healing 
temperature and increase the healing effect of the materials. It is now 
widely acknowledged that low healing temperature can make such 
materials more convenient and practical, and that high mechanical 
strength after healing is desired for their application. However, better 
healing effect usually requires the presence of soft chains in the material, 
which in turn weakens its mechanical properties. Incorporation of DA 
adducting structure has been proved capable of achieving higher me­
chanical strength for the self-healing material. In this aspect, our group 
had previously reported the successful synthesis of a series of self- 
healing coatings with DA adducts [25,26]. Another outstanding chal­
lenge existing in the self-healing technology is the healing of wide 
scratches. Currently, the healing of scratch is usually limited to cracks 
with narrow width (<50 μm), not those with large sizes [21,24,27,28]. 
However, the occurrence of different sizes of damage is unavoidable in 
practical situation. Therefore, how to achieve self-healing of a wide 
scratch has become one of the key considerations in designation and 
optimization of the repairing approach. 
Shape memory polymer (SMP) has attracted numerous interests due 
to its ability to memorize its original shape after certain stimulus, such as 
heat, humidity, light, pH, electricity, etc., was applied. Conceptually, 
SMP can be divided into two types: one-way and two-way SMP [29–34]. 
Owing to its appealing reversibility, the two-way SMP appears as 
overwhelming alternative for the one-way SMP. However, traditional 
two-way SMP (also known as quasi two-way SMP) needs an additional

smart robots and medical materials. Therefore, many methods have 
been explored to solve this problem, making the production of shape 
memory more convenient and reliable. For example, Zhang and col­

external stress-free two-way SMP and verified its shape-memory effect at 
both micro and macro levels. In their work, a self-healing elastomer was 
prepared and further used for healing of wide cracks, with an excellent 
healing effect and high strength of about 27.5 MPa achieved. It can be 
seen that, by introducing special shape memory effect to the self-healing 
polymer, the elastomer material with an artificial wide scratch was 
found to shrink at a proper temperature and then healed automatically 
at a higher temperature due to the reversibility of dynamic bonds, which 
finally led to the self-healing of wide scratch. 
Herein, we designed and synthesized a novel self-healing poly­
urethane elastomer (denoted as PUDA) containing DA bonds and poly­
caprolactone (PCL) soft segments. The developed elastomer exhibited 
not only excellent healing ability of wide scratch (80−100 μm), but also 
super mechanical strength (up to 32.88 MPa). The elastomer also 
exhibited an excellent healing efficiency of mechanical properties. It 
demonstrated a more than 100 % recovery of mechanical strength after 
healing, with its tensile strength reaching up to 34.58 MPa, which out­
performed most currently available self-healing elastomers. 
2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Materials 
Furfuryl alcohol (FA; 98 %), bismaleimide (BMI; 99 %), hexam­
ethylene diisocyanate (HDI; 99 %), polycaprolactone diol (PCL; Mn =
2000; 99 %), 1, 4-butanediol (BDO; 99 %), trimethylolpropane tris(3- 
mercaptopropionate) (TMPMP; 95 %), dibutyltin dilaurate (DBTDL; 95 
%), acetone, dimethylformamide (DMF), diethyl ether, were purchased 
from Heowns. FA was distilled at 78◦C under reduced pressure. PCL2000 
was dehydrated in oven at 60◦C for 6 h prior to use. Other reagents and 
solvents were used as received. 
2.2. Synthesis of diols containing DA bonds (DA-diol) 
0.024 mol BMI and 0.01 mol FA were first dissolved in 100 mL DMF. 
Then the mixture was stirred at 60◦C until the absorption of BMI at 696 
cm−1 on the Fourier transform infrared spectra (FTIR) disappeared. The 
resultant was finally precipitated with diethyl ether, and filtered to 
obtain a pale yellow solid. The synthesis route was shown in Scheme 1. 
2.3. Synthesis of PUDA and the control PUBDO 
Typically, 0.01 mol PCL and 0.022 mol HDI was mixed with 3‰ 
DBTDL and dissolved in acetone. The solution was then heated up to 
60◦C and stirred for 1 h to yield prepolymer 1. After that, 0.0048 mol 
DA-diol was added in as a chain extender and stirred for 1 h to yield 
prepolymer 2. Afterwards, 0.0048 mol TMPMP was added in as a 
crosslinking agent until the FTIR absorption peak at 2270 cm−1 dis­
appeared, indicating that the -NCO group was completely consumed. 
The resultant was poured into a PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene) mold 
with different shapes (dumbbell, circle, and strip) at room temperature 
for 24 h to remove the solvent, and crosslinked PUDA samples were 
finally obtained. The average molecular weight between crosslinks (Mc) 
for the crosslinked polymers is calculated to be 3915.6. 
To demonstrate the critical role of DA bonds, control sample without 
DA bonds (denoted as PUBDO) was prepared following similar steps, 
except that BDO was used as the diol instead of DA-diol. The monomer 
ratio used during the synthesis of PUDA and PUBDO was shown in 
Table 1. 
2.4. Processing program adopted for attainment of stress-free two-way 
shape memory effect 
To endow the synthesized PUDA elastomer stress-free two-way shape 
memory effect, a special designed treatment procedure was adopted in 
our experiments according to literature [35] with a little modification. 
To be specific, the dumbbell-shaped sample of crosslinked PUDA elas­
tomer was first heated at 60◦C for 10 min to ensure the melting of the 
PCL soft segments. The sample was then stretched to two times its 
original length and kept at room temperature for 30 min, followed by 
cooling down under constant strain in a refrigerator for 10 min until it 
was no longer shrink under the applied force. Finally, the sample was 
heated at 60◦C for 10 min and then cooled down to room temperature 
again. It was found that the obtained sample that denoted as pro­
grammed PUDA or programmed PUBDO could realize stress-free two-­
way shape memory effect and automatically shrink under heating. 
2.5. Characterization 
2.5.1. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) 
FTIR measurements were used to monitor the reaction process and 
characterize the chemical structure of the obtained products. Depending 
on the physical state of the synthetic products, two methods were 
adopted for sample preparation for FTIR testing. For solid resultant, it 
was first crushed and grinded with KBr powder, and then pressed into 
circular sheet. As for liquid resultant, it was evenly dropped onto KBr 
salt slice. The spectra were collected using a Bruker Vector 70 FTIR 
spectrometer. Each sample was run for 24 scans with a scanning range of 
400−4000 cm−1 and a resolution of 4 cm−1. 
2.5.2. Proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1H NMR) 
The 1H NMR spectroscopy were recorded on Bruker UAVANCE III 
400 MHz spectrometer with DMSO-d6 (2.50 ppm) as solvents and tet­
ramethylsilane as the internal standard at room temperature.

2.5.4. Rheological measurement 
Rheology testing were carried out using a Discovery HR-2 rheometer

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Synthesis and characterization of PUDA and PUBDO 
To synthesize PUDA, DA-diols were first synthesized via reaction 
between BMI and FA at 60◦C for 24 h (Scheme 1). The reaction progress 
was monitored by FTIR method and the structure of product was char­
acterized by 1H NMR. As shown in Fig. 1(a), (b), two new absorption 
bands at 1774 cm−1 and 1188 cm−1 that assigned to the DA adducting 
structure occurred, with its intensity increasing with the extension of the 
reaction time. Additionally, the characteristic peak at 696 cm−1 of BMI 
disappeared, indicating that the raw materials had fully converted into 
product as the reaction proceeded. The 1H NMR spectrum with nine 
groups of characteristic peaks verifies well the structure of product, and 
the correspondence between the peak and the chemical group was 
marked in Fig. 1(c). According to above analysis, it could be concluded 
that the DA adducts (DA-diols) were successfully synthesized. 
Afterwards, HDI was reacted with PCL 2000 to obtain PU prepolymer 
1, followed by addition of DA-diols to generate PUDA elastomer, which 
was finally crosslinked by TMPMP to obtain crosslinked PUDA elastomer 
[37] (Scheme 2). The reactions were also monitored and the structure of 
the products was examined via FTIR method. As shown in Fig. 2, after 
HDI reacted with PCL and DA-diols for 1 h successively, the absorption 
band at 2270 cm−1 corresponding to the -NCO bonds significantly 
decreased stepwise, indicating that HDI had reacted with PCL and 
DA-diols. In addition, after crosslinked by TMPMP for 1 h, the absorp­
tion peak of -NCO at 2270 cm−1 disappeared completely, implying the 
-NCO groups had been consumed entirely by the thiol monomers. The 
PUBDO system behaved in a similar way as shown in Fig. 3. The dif­
ference between PUDA and PUBDO is the diol monomers, one is DA-diol 
while the other is BDO diol. According to the published work [25,26], 
the peak at 3067 cm−1 indicates the DA structure and this peak is not 
obvious due to the adjacent strong peak of N–H at 3355 cm−1. How­
ever, the spectrum of PUDA after the reaction has obvious peak at 3067 
cm−1 while the peak of PUBDO is weak. 
3.2. Shape memory effect of PU elastomers 
The self-healing of PU elastomers towards wide scratches depends on 
the material shrinkage that derived from the shape memory effect and 
the flow (diffusion) of polymer chains that caused by the breakage of DA 
bonds. Therefore, shrinkage behavior of the PUDA elastomer was first 
measured to probe its shape-memory ability and confirm whether it 
could exhibit stress-free two-way shape memory. 
Firstly, one-way shape-memory effect [38] was examined with the 
PUDA elastomer. The specimens were heated at 60◦C for 10 min, after 
which they were molded into different shapes (Deform 1 and Deform 2, 
as shown in Fig. 4). Subsequently, the deformed specimens were cooled 
down to 5◦C until their shapes were fixed. After that, the specimens were 
heated up to 60◦C again and kept at that temperature for a period of 
time. As expected, the shape of the specimens converted quickly back to 
their original shape-a flat strip, indicating that the elastomer specimens 
could exhibit one-way shape memory effect after PCL soft blocks

It has been reported that PU elastomers containing PCL soft segments 
could be trained to acquire two-way shape-memory effect when they

In order to study the rheological behavior in high temperature, fre­
quency sweeps at 25◦C (room temperature) and 130◦C were tested. 
According to the previous analysis of DSC, 127◦C is the most violent 
period of DA bonds’ breakage, most of DA bonds has been fully broken at 
130◦C and the corresponding rheological behavior is similar to that of 
healing. And sample at 25◦C worked as a reference of original sample 
which has full DA bonds. Therefore the influence of DA bonds’ breakage 
on the rheological behavior and healing mechanism at high temperature 
can be studied through the frequency sweep tests at 25◦C and 130◦C. 
The frequency sweep at 25◦C (Fig. 9b) showed that the storage modulus 
was much higher than the loss modulus, implying that the PUDA 
structure is a crosslinked network. By contrast, the frequency sweep at 
130◦C (Fig. 9c) displayed that the storage modulus was nearly the same 
as the loss modulus at a frequency of around 0. Moreover, the storage 
modulus at 130◦C was decreased by one order of magnitude compared 
with that at 25◦C, which could be resulted from the breakage of DA 
bonds during the retro-DA reaction. The table in Fig. 9 was the sum­
marized data of storage modulus (G’) at 25◦C and 130◦C which showed 
that the great gaps between the two modulus. These data indicate that 
the trained PUDA exhibits the behavior of elastomer at 25◦C, and dis­
plays the behavior of liquid at 130◦C, which were due to the thermal 
reversibility of DA reactions. 
The healing mechanism, which mainly depends on the polymer flow 
caused by retro-DA reaction, was further confirmed by reversible solu­
tion experiments (Fig. 10). During the experiment, a piece of PUDA 
elastomer was first soaked in DMF at room temperature (25◦C) for 30 
min, and the sample was observed to be just slightly swollen, indicating 
there must be crosslinked network existing in the PUDA sample. When 
the swollen sample was continued to be heated up to 120◦C for another 
30 min, it was dissolved into a transparent solution, implying the 
crosslinked network in PUDA had been broken due to the retro-DA re­
action. However, when the transparent solution was cooled down and 
kept at 60◦C for 7 d, the solvent was completely removed and the so­
lution turned into a solid, which could not be dissolved again after being 
immersed in the solvent at room temperature, confirming that DA bonds 
were rebuilt and the solid had converted back to a crosslinked state 
again. 
The mechanism for self-healing of wider scratch was further illus­
trated and the schematic diagram were shown in Fig. 11. Firstly, heating 
at 60◦C (which is higher than the melting temperature of PCL but lower 
than the temperature required for retro-DA reaction) caused the melting 
of PCL crystallites and the shrinking of the materials in a millimeter 
scale, similar to that shown in Fig. 6. Therefore, the scratch width 
became narrower, which is favorable for the subsequent healing. When 
the material was heated at 120◦C for a period of time, the retro-DA re­
action took place and the DA bonds began to break, causing the flow of 
polymer chains to become more accessible. Afterwards, the soft seg­
ments of broken PUDA began to flow into the narrow scratch and 
enabled the self-healing of the shrunken scratch. In the end, when the 
material was cooled down to room temperature, the PCL soft segments 
were recrystallized and the DA bonds were rebuilt, leading to the rees­
tablishment of the crosslinked network.

As shown in Fig. 12, PUDA or PUBDO sample was first cut into two 
pieces, which were brought into contact and heated at 60◦C for 1 h to 
allow the rejoining of the two broken pieces and the closure of the 
scratch. Subsequently, the temperature was increased to 120◦C for 3 h, 
and the sample was re-stretched after being cooled down to room tem­
perature. It was observed that PUDA sample exhibited excellent self- 
healing effect as it could be re-stretched after healing. However, the 
PUBDO sample showed poor tensile property and broken immediately 
once stretched, implying the scratch in PUBDO sample was not healed. 
The results of the comparative experiments demonstrate that the two- 
way shape-memory effect allows the scratch to close but the healing is 
caused by DA bonds. 
To further confirm the role of two-way shape-memory effect in the 
healing of wide scratch, a scratch with sizes of 80−100 μm was gener­
ated in situ on the programmed or unprogrammed PUDA film surface 
using a razor blade. The specimen was then placed on a heating stage, 
and the temperature was gradually increased from 30◦C to 110◦C to 
allow healing. The changes of scratch at different temperatures were 
monitored [39] using an optical microscope (Fig. 13). It could be easily 
seen that, when the temperature increased, the wide scratch on the 
programmed PUDA sample became narrower and finally disappeared 
after heated at 110◦C for 6 min. However, the scratch width in the un­
programmed specimen didn’t change much and only slightly healed 
under heating. The results reveal that the stress-free two-way 
shape-memory effect derived from programmed treatment is crucial and 
indispensable for the healing of wide scratch. 
The mechanical properties of the original PUDA elastomer material 
and the healed PUDA specimen was studied by tensile testing. As shown 
in Fig. 14, the tensile strength of the original trained PUDA sample is 
measured to be as high as 32.88 MPa, which is super high than those 
self-healing elastomers previously reported. The excellent mechanical 
strength is believed to mainly result from the introduced DA adducting 
structure. In fact, it was found in our experiments that the tensile 
strength of the PUDA elastomer increased with the increase of the 
content of DA adducting structure in the PU network. Fig. 15 visually 
demonstrated the high strength of our synthesized PUDA specimen. 
With a piece of rectangular PUDA film specimen (8 cm in length, 4 cm in 
width and 1 mm in thickness), a steel weighting up to 5 Kg can be easily 
lifted, and the film was not damaged after the lifting test. 
To investigate the recovery of mechanical property, a dumbbell- 
shaped PUDA specimen was cut into two pieces, which was brought 
into contact and subsequently placed in an oven at 110◦C for 1 ~ 4 h. 
The specimen was further cooled down to 60◦C and kept for 3 h to allow 
the regeneration of the DA bands. Eventually, the broken specimen was 
observed to be completely rejoined. Moreover, the tensile strength of the 
healed specimen was observed to increase with the healing time at 
110◦C (as shown in Fig. 14), and it was recorded to climb to 34.58 MPa 
after 4 h’s heating, which is even beyond its original value. 
4. Conclusions 
In summary, a novel self-healing polyurethane elastomer containing 
PCL soft blocks and dynamic AD adducting structure was synthesized. 
The two-way shape-memory effect of the PUDA elastomer and the 
thermal reversibility of DA adducting structure were confirmed. Also, 
the healing mechanism was explored. The results show that the elas­
tomer displayed unprecedented tensile strength and excellent healing 
performance towards wide scratches. The tensile strength of original 
crosslinked PUDA reached up to 32.88 MPa, which is super high 
compared to that of self-healing elastomers previously reported. More­
over, it could be recovered by up to 113 % of the initial strength after 
healing. The trained PUDA elastomer also exhibited excellent healing 
performance towards wide scratches: a scratch with width of 80−100 
μm could be completely recovered after heating at 110◦C for just 6 min. 
Benefiting from its outstanding self-healing ability and superior tensile 
strength, the developed PUDA elastomer demonstrates quite promising 
perspective in material science and engineering field, especially for the 
protection of surface coating.
