A self-healing and recyclable polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder 
adduct synthesized from carbon dioxide and furfuryl amine 
Peixuan Wu,a,b,c Haiyang Cheng,*,a,c Xinchao Wang,a,b,c Ruhui Shi,a,b,c Chao Zhang,a,c Masahiko Arai a,c 
and Fengyu Zhao*,a,b,c
The fabrication of self-healing and recyclable CO 2-based polymeric materials meets the requirement of sustainable 
development. By introducing reversible hydrogen bonds and heat-responsive Diels-Alder bonds, a new, environmentally 
friendly, self-healing and recyclable polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder adduct (PUUa-DA) was successfully synthesized using CO2 
and furfuryl amine as renewable feedstocks. PUUa-DA was semi-crystalline and exhibited high thermal stability, 5% weight 
loss temperature being above 220 ºC. Moreover, the PUUa-DA with cross-linked structure showed a tensile stress of 18.5 
MPa, a modulus of 151 MPa and an elongation at break of 136%. The dynamic Diels-Alder bonds and hydrogen bonds provide 
the self-healing response and contribute to 94% recovery in tensile strength upon heating. Therefore, PUUa-DA will have 
potential applications as a functional material in a wide range of practical fields. The synthesis of PUUa-DA using CO 2 and 
bio-based furfuryl amine will be of importance in green and sustainable chemistry.
Introduction 
The progresses achieved in polymer materials have benefited the 
human life in these decades. The development of self-healing and 
recyclable materials is of significance and a major challenge in 
polymer science.1 Thus, much efforts have been invested so far and 
some progresses have been obtained in preparing the self-healing 
and recycling polymeric materials such as polyolefins, polyesters, 
polyamides, and polyurethanes. 2, 3 It is prerequisite to easily build, 
break and rebuild dynamic covalent bonds and/or noncovalent 
supramolecular interactions in the polymeric structure for obtaining 
the desired self-healing and recycling properties. Dynamic covalent 
bonds such as disulfide, 4, 5  B−O bonds, 6, 7  imine bonds, 8, 9  and 
Diels−Alder bonds10-14 and noncovalent supramolecular interactions 
such as hydrogen bonding, 15, 16 metal−ligand interaction, 17, 18 ionic 
interaction,19, 20  and π−π stacking interactions 21, 22  have been 
frequently used in the design of self-healing and recyclable polymers.
Polyurethanes and polyureas are among the most important 
polymeric materials that have versatile properties, so they have been 
used in huge volumes in many industrial and commodity products. 
The traditional synthesis of polyurethanes and polyureas usually 
invokes isocyanate chemistry, which uses toxic multifunctional 
isocyanates. Therefore, the use of CO 2 as a nontoxic and renewable 
feedstock for the synthesis of polymers have attracted growing 
attention from both academic and industrial communities. 23 In 
recent years, various CO 2-based polyurethanes and polyureas have 
been synthesized through direct and indirect routes, in which the 
former uses CO 2 as a monomer and the latter uses CO 2-derived 
building blocks. 23 CO 2 is reacted with appropriate reagents and 
catalysts to produce polyureas. 24-36 CO 2-derived monomers such as 
cyclic carbonate, 37-40 carbamate, 41, 42  and urea 43-45 are used to 
synthesize polyurethanes and polyureas. So far, a few kinds of self-
healing and recyclable CO 2-based polymeric materials have been 
reported.10, 46-48 In 2016, Caillol and coworkers developed thermo-
responsive CO 2-based polyurethane by converting furfuryl glycidyl 
ether into cyclic carbonate derivative and mixing it with dimaleimide-
terminated polypropylene glycol and appropriate diamine. The 
resulted polyurethane can return from a solid state to a liquid state 
due to the break of Diels-Alder adducts, which is desired for many 
thermo-reversible materials applications. 49 In 2019, Wu and 
coworkers reported the CO 2‑based healable thermoplastic 
elastomers via a tandem synthetic strategy, which exhibits 
characteristic properties of thermoplastic elastomers with a Young’s 
modulus of up to 10.1 MPa. Moreover, robust self-healing ability 
could be endowed via addition of a small amount of boronic ester as 
dynamic cross-linker.46 In our recent work, a self-healing CO 2-based 
polyurea was fabricated via reasonable regulating the strength, 
arrangement and density of hydrogen bonds. The synthesized CO 2-
based polyurea is self-healable at room temperature and the 
superior mechanical properties of tensile strength (6.6 MPa) and 
toughness (28.7 MJ m -3) can be recovered 95%. 47 In the present 
work, we have further attempted to design a self-healing and 
recyclable CO 2-based polymer with high strength properties by 
introducing stronger cross-linking bonds via Diels-Alder reaction, in 
which stronger dynamic covalent bonds would endow superior 
mechanical strength and stability. The Diels-Alder reaction is one of

Herein, the authors will report the synthesis of a self-healing and 
recyclable CO 2-based polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder adduct from 
CO2 and furfuryl amine. Compared to the previously reported CO 2 
route,56, 57 this new route is greener and eco-friendlier, because no 
isocyanates are used, but CO 2 is used as comonomer with a bio-
based furfuryl amine. Linear polyurethane-urea was prepared by the 
polyaddition reaction between furfuryl amine cyclocarbonate 
derivative and oligourea. Then polyurethane-urea was cross-linked 
by the Diels-Alder reaction between furfuryl and 4,4-
bismaleimidodiphenylmethane (BIM). The mechanical properties 
(modulus, tensile stress and elongation at break) of polyurethane-
urea Diels-Alder adduct are excellent due to the combination of 
ordered and disordered hydrogen bonding interactions and the 
appropriate amounts of crosslinked Diels-Alder groups. Meanwhile, 
polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder adduct showed excellent self-healing 
capability and recyclability. The present green route is effective to 
build strong and tough self-healing and recyclable polymers.
Experimental
Materials
Furfuryl amine (FA, 98%), 4,7,10-trioxa-1,13-tridecanediamine (TTD, 
98%), epichlorohydrin (ECH, 99%), tetrabutylammonium bromide 
(TBAB, 99%), lithium bromide (LiBr, 99%), and 4,4-
bismaleimidodiphenylmethane (BIM, 96%) were purchased from 
Tokyo Chem. Ind. and used as received. Poly(propylene glycol) bis(2-
aminopropyl ether) (PEA, number-average molecular weight (M n) 
1542 g/mol based on Matrix-assisted laser desorption and ionization 
time-of-flight mass spectra), N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF, 99.5%), 
chloroform-d (99.8%), sodium hydroxide (96%), and magnesium 
sulfate (99.5%) were purchased from Aladdin and used as received. 
Carbon dioxide (99.999%) and nitrogen (99.999%) were purchased 
from Changchun Juyang Gas Company.
Synthesis of oligourea (OUa)
4,7,10-Trioxa-1,13-tridecanediamine (40 g, 0.182 mol) was 
introduced into the 50 mL autoclave and it was sealed and purged 
with N2 three times to remove air. Then, the autoclave was heated 
to 180 ºC and CO2 was introduced up to 11.5 MPa. The reaction was 
run at a stirring speed of 400 rpm for 6 h. After the reaction was 
finished, the autoclave was cooled to ambient temperature and CO 2 
was vented slowly. The product was dried in N 2 flow at 140 ºC for 3 
h and then under vacuum at 80 ºC for 24 h (38.5 g, 86% yield). The 
Mn of oligourea (OUa) was 1550 g mol-1 based on the 1H NMR data.58
Synthesis of polyurea (PUa)
Oligourea (25 g, 0.0162 mol) was introduced into a 250 mL three-
necked flask with a mechanical stirrer. At a constant CO 2 flow rate 
(50 mL/min) and at a constant stirring speed (170 rpm), the reaction 
was carried out at 200 ºC for 1 h. Then the temperature was 
increased to 250 ºC, and the reaction was conducted at this 
temperature for 1.16 h. After the reaction, the product was cooled 
to room temperature, collected, and stored (20.5 g, 82% yield). The 
Mn of polyurea (PUa) was 4952 g mol-1 based on the 1H NMR data.36
Synthesis of polyurethane-urea (PUUa)
Oligourea (31 g, 20 mmol), bis(cyclic carbonate) furfuryl amine (5.95 
g, 20 mmol) and DMF (30 mL) were introduced into a 100 mL round-
bottom flask. Then the reaction mixture was heated to 150 ºC and 
stirred for 2 h. After the reaction, DMF was removed by distillation 
and the product of polyurethane-urea (PUUa) was collected and 
dried under vacuum at 60 ºC for 24 h (36.5 g, 98% yield).
Synthesis of cross-linked polyurethane-urea (PUUa-DA)
Polyurethane-urea (18.5 g, 10 mmol) and 4,4-bismaleimido-
diphenylmethane (1.79 g, 5 mmol) were dissolved in 50 mL DMF. The 
resulting solution was poured onto a Teflon mold and dried in air at 
60 ºC for 24 h. The polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder adduct (PUUa-DA) 
was obtained by further drying under vacuum at 60 ºC for 24 h (19.3 
g, 95% yield).
Synthesis of cross-linked polyurethane-urea from TTD and PEA
4,7,10-Trioxa-1,13-tridecanediamine (6.6 g, 30 mmol), bis(cyclic 
carbonate) furfuryl amine (8.91 g, 30 mmol) and DMF (30 mL) were 
introduced into a 100 mL round-bottom flask. Then the reaction 
mixture was heated to 150 ºC and stirred for 2 h. After the reaction, 
DMF was removed by distillation and the product was collected and 
dried under vacuum at 60 ºC for 24 h. Then, the product (12.18 g, 
23.5 mmol) and 4,4-bismaleimidodiphenylmethane (4.22 g, 11.7 
mmol) were dissolved in 50 mL DMF. The resulting solution was 
poured onto a Teflon mold and dried in air and vacuum at 60 ºC for 
24 h each. The product was donated as TTD-DA. Similarly, PEA-DA 
were prepared from PEA as similar process.
Characterization
1H NMR (400 MHz) was measured on a Bruker AV-400 at room 
temperature with CDCl 3 as a solvent and tetramethylsilane as an 
internal reference.
Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were measured by 
Thermo Scientific Nicolet iS50 spectrometer with a DTGS detector at 
a region of 4000-400 cm -1. Temperature-Dependent Fourier-
transform infrared (TD-FTIR) spectra were obtained by Bruker 
INVENIO spectrometer equipped with a diamond ATR crystal in the 
range between 4000 and 600 cm -1. The sample was heated from 30 
to 120 ºC at 3 ºC min-1 and FTIR spectra were collected at the desired 
temperatures.
Thermo gravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning 
calorimetry (DSC) were performed on TA-Q50 and TA-Q100 
instruments, respectively. Samples (about 5~10 mg) were heated 
from 50 to 500 ºC for TGA and from -50 to 150 ºC for DSC at a heating 
rate of 10 ºC min-1 in nitrogen atmosphere.

Synthesis and characterization of polyurethane-urea (PUUa)
PUUa was prepared from bis(cyclic carbonate) furfuryl amine 
(BCCFA) and oligourea (OUa) by polyaddition reaction as illustrated 
in Scheme 1. Either BCCFA or OUa was synthesized with CO 2. Bio-
based furfurylamine was first converted into the corresponding 
diglycidyl furfurylamine (DGFA) by reacting with epichlorohydrin, 
and then DGFA was converted into the corresponding difunctional 
cyclic carbonate of BCCFA by reacting with CO2. The 1H NMR spectra 
of DGFA and BCCFA are in accordance with the ones given in 
literature (Fig. S1†).49 
OUa was synthesized from 4,7,10-trioxa-1,13-tridecanediamine 
(TTD) and CO 2 using polycondensation reaction. 59 The structure of 
OUa prepared was confirmed by FTIR and 1H NMR. As shown in Fig. 
1a, the formation of urea linkage was confirmed by absorption bands 
of -C(=O)- stretching vibration at 1612 cm -1 and -NH bending 
vibration at 1590 cm -1, in addition to the absorption band of -NH 
stretching vibration at 3321 cm -1 (Fig. 1a). 60 1H NMR showed that a 
new signal at 3.2 ppm appeared in OUa, as compared with TTD, 
which was the characteristic signal of -CH 2- protons linked to urea 
group (Fig. S2 †). Moreover, the molecular weight of OUa was 
calculated by 1H NMR to be about 1550 g mol -1.58 To compare the 
mechanical properties, polyurea (PUa) was prepared by the further 
reaction of OUa and CO 2 at a high temperature of 250 ºC to get a 
reference polymer with a high molecular weight (4952 g mol -1) 
(Scheme S1†).36 The FTIR spectrum of PUa was similar to that of OUa; 
the absorption bands of C=O stretching vibration at 1610 cm-1 and N-
H bending vibration at 1587 cm -1 were ascribed to the strong and 
ordered hydrogen bonded urea groups of PUa (Fig. S3†). 
OUa cannot be pressed into a shape of bar suitable to measure the 
mechanical properties, but its amino groups at both ends are 
reactive and OUa can dissolve in DMF to react with BCCFA. PUa could 
not dissolve in DMF.36 Therefore, OUa was used to react with BCCFA 
to synthesize PUUa. The reaction was detected by FTIR (Fig. 1). A 
strong absorption band at 1793 cm -1 ascribed to the C=O stretching 
vibration in cyclic carbonate groups of BCCFA was weakened in 1 h

adduct (PUUa-DA)
The Diels-Alder reaction between furan and maleimide groups 
could build dynamic cross-linked thermoplastics with thermo-
reversible properties. 61 PUUa has a furan group, so self-healing 
materials can be constructed by Diels-Alder reaction. PUUa Diels-
Alder adduct (PUUa-DA) was synthesized from PUUa and BIM. Fig. 3 
shows two new characteristic absorption bands at 1710 and 1773

which means that the cross-linked PUUa-DA was successfully 
synthesized. The weight ratio of C=O in urea group of PUUa-DA is 
11.8 wt.%; i.e., the consumption of CO2 in PUUa-DA is 16.1 wt.%. The 
amount of bio-based furylamine in PUUa-DA is 4.7 wt.%. With 
increasing temperature from 30 to 120 ºC, the absorption bands of 
C=O stretching vibration at 1609 cm -1 and N-H bending vibration at 
1589 cm-1 in the urea groups were gradually weakened. Meanwhile, 
the intensity of the N-H stretching vibration band at 3317 cm -1 was 
gradually decreased, while the absorption bands of the “free” C=O 
stretching vibration at 1658 cm -1 and the “free” N-H bending 
vibration at 1542 cm -1 gradually became stronger. These results 
indicated the strong and ordered hydrogen bonding of urea groups 
changed to the weak and disordered one with increasing 
temperature. After cooling to 30 ºC for 24 h, the FTIR spectrum 
became similar to that of the starting material of PUUa-DA, indicating 
the strong and ordered hydrogen bonding was reformed (Fig. S4†). 
Furthermore, the characteristic peak of maleimide at 690 cm -1 did 
not appear at 30 ºC, indicating maleimide was reacted with furan ring 
through the Diels-Alder reaction. When PUUa-DA was heated to 70 
ºC, the absorption band at 690 cm -1 ascribed to maleimide was 
observed, indicating that the retro-Diels-Alder reaction occurred. 
The reaction was also verified by differential scanning calorimetry 
(DSC) in the first heating scan (Fig. S5†). PUUa-DA showed a 
significant broad endothermic temperature at round 108 ºC, which 
was due to the retro-Diels-Alder reaction.
Non-isocyanate polyurethane is usually obtained by the reaction 
of diamine with difunctional cyclic carbonate. For comparison, OUa 
was replaced by TTD or PEA to react with BCCFA, the obtained 
product was further reacted with BIM, and the corresponding 
polymer (TTD-DA or PEA-DA) was prepared (Scheme S2†). From FTIR 
spectra, compared with PUUa-DA, there is no strong and ordered 
hydrogen bonding in TTD-DA and PEA-DA, because TTD-DA and PEA-
DA have only carbamate group but no urea group (Fig. S3†).

Mechanical properties
The mechanical properties were characterized using a uniaxial 
tensile test at a strain rate of 20 mm min -1 (Fig. 6, Table S3†). OUa 
obtained from TTD and CO 2 was too fragile to be characterized due 
to the low molecular weight (1550 g mol -1). When OUa was further 
reacted with CO 2, the obtained high molecular weight PUa (4952 g 
mol-1) was still a typical of stiff polymer with a high Young’s modulus 
of 527 MPa and an elongation at break of 3.2%. Both OUa and PUa 
are semi-crystalline and have the strong, ordered and 3D network 
crosslinked intermolecular hydrogen bonds (Fig. 1a, 5, Fig. S4†).25, 36 
When TTD was reacted with BCCFA, the viscous liquid product 
obtained was further reacted with BIM and the TTD-DA so produced 
was a fragile gel; as a results, its mechanical properties were unable 
to measure, in which short TTD segment caused too high crosslinking 
density of DA adduct. To get tough and strong material, we prepared 
PUUa. First, either BCCFA or OUa was prepared from CO 2. Then 
BCCFA was reacted with OU. The PUUa so formed was found to be a 
though thermoplastic with a tensile stress of 15.9 MPa, an elongation 
at break of 46% and a Young’s modulus of 206 MPa. Compared to 
PUa, the enhanced tough of PUUa was caused by the introduction of 
the irregular structure of furan and the formation of carbamate 
group, reducing the crystallization due to an increase in the 
disordered hydrogen bonding interactions. To further enhance the 
toughness and get a self-healing action, PUUa was further reacted 
with BIM though the reversible Diels-Alder reaction, and a cross-

while the carbamate groups provided the weak and disordered 
hydrogen bonding interaction. The combination of the ordered and 
disordered hydrogen bonding interactions and the

favorable for the improvement of mechanical properties. In order to 
further examine the influence of the strong hydrogen bonding on the 
mechanical properties, a polyetheramine of PEA with a Mn similar to 
OUa was selected to replace OUa. The PEA-DA prepared exhibited a 
tensile stress of 0.085 MPa, an elongation at break of 58% and a

PEA-DA and PUUa-DA was due to the fact that PEA could not provide 
strong and ordered hydrogen bonding for the former (Fig. S3†).

To examine the macroscopic self-healing behavior, a rectangular 
specimen of PUUa-DA was cut into two pieces and the fresh 
surfaces were brought into contact immediately ( Fig. 7) . Then, it 
was processed by thermally treatment at 120 ºC for 10 min and 60 
ºC for 24 h to allow the retro-Diels-Alder and Diels-Alder reactions, 
respectively. After that, the healed specimen can be twist and bent 
without breaking. At 120 ºC, retro-Diels-Alder reaction happened 
very quickly and PUUa-DA was transformed to PUUa, which is a 
thermoplastic, then PUUa melted due to its melting temperatures 
(84 or 102 ºC) are lower. Then the broken hydrogen bond in PUUa 
was recombined, and the crosslinked Diels-Alder groups were 
reformed by Diels-Alder reactions at 60 ºC for 24 h. However, at the 
healing temperature lower than 108 ºC, retro-Diels-Alder reaction is 
insufficient, although the melted OUa segment make the molecular 
chains move easily, but PUUa-DA is still a thermosetting material 
without melting, so PUUa-DA cannot be healed effectively at 
temperature below 108 ºC.  In addition, the self-healing process

the crack was slightly closed during heating at temperatures lower

seen on the surface after healing for 2 min. Furthermore, the self-

Conclusions
In this work, we have reported a green route to synthesize bio-based 
non-isocyanate polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder adduct using CO2 as a 
nontoxic and renewable feedstock. It was used as co-monomer with 
a bio-based furfuryl amine. First, bio-based furfurylamine was first 
converted into the diglycidyl furfurylamine, and then diglycidyl 
furfurylamine was converted into bis(cyclic carbonate) furfuryl 
amine by reacting with CO2. Second, oligourea was synthesized from 
4,7,10-trioxa-1,13-tridecanediamine and CO 2 using 
polycondensation reaction. Oligourea was fragile polymer due to the 
strong, ordered and 3D network crosslinked intermolecular 
hydrogen bonds. Third, polyurethane-urea was prepared by the 
reaction of oligourea and bis(cyclic carbonate) furfuryl amine. 
Polyurethane-urea was a though thermoplastic with a tensile stress 
of 15.9 MPa, an elongation at break of 46% and a Young’s modulus 
of 206 MPa. The larger toughness, compared to the oligourea, 
resulted from the irregular structure of furan and the formation of 
carbamate group reduced the crystallization and ordered hydrogen 
bonding interactions. Finally, polyurethane-urea was further reacted 
with 4,4-bismaleimidodiphenylmethane though the reversible Diels-
Alder reaction, and a cross-linking polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder 
adduct was obtained. Polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder adduct 
exhibited as a tough and self-healing thermosetting material, 
compared with polyurethane-urea, the Young’s modulus decreased 
to 151 MPa, but the tensile stress increased to 18.5 MPa and the 
elongation at break increased to 136 %. The combination of the 
ordered and disordered hydrogen bonding interactions and the 
appropriate amounts of crosslinked Diels-Alder groups in 
polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder adduct make it exhibit excellent 
mechanical and self-healing properties. The healing efficiency is 94% 
when it was healed at 120 ºC for 10 min and then healed at 60 ºC for 
24 h. In addition, the polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder adduct showed 
good recyclability and could be remolded via hot-pressing. The 
successful synthesis of polyurethane-urea Diels-Alder adduct with 
great performance would further expand the potential application of 
environmental-friendly non-isocyanate polyurethane.
